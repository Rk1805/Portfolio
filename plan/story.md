### 3.1 Pet Agents — `/work/pet-agents`

*macOS · Swift · on-device AI · 2026*

**Hook.** Desktop pets that actually do your chores. Click a pixel ninja, type "play Blinding Lights" or "remind me in 20 minutes to stretch", and it happens on your Mac.

**Problem.** Desktop pets are charming and useless. Assistants are useful and charmless — and most send what you type to a server. I wanted one thing that was both: a character you like having on screen, and an assistant that runs on your own machine.

**Insight.** If the pet is the interface, the assistant stops feeling like a tool. And if the model runs on-device, it needs no API keys and no account — you build the app and it works.

**Build.**
- A native macOS app in Swift. Pets walk along the bottom of the screen and on top of windows, can be picked up and dragged, and perform signature moves.
- Free-form requests are understood by Apple Intelligence on the Mac itself. Built-in commands work even with it switched off.
- Missions it can carry out: open apps, play a song on Spotify, set reminders and calendar entries, type into other apps, open a WhatsApp chat by contact name, toggle dark mode. Push-to-talk voice input, plus built-in Hinglish commands.
- 27 characters across two packs. Every animation frame is drawn from scratch by a skeleton rig — shoulders, elbows, hips, knees — with inverse kinematics for things like bringing a hand to the mouth. Frames are 192×192 pixel art at 10 fps, generated by a Python pipeline.
- Builds with Apple's free Command Line Tools only; no Xcode, Homebrew, Node or API keys.

**Result.** A working app, built and tested on macOS 26 on Apple Silicon. A solo passion project, not released yet — so no usage claims are made.

### 3.2 Phishing Email Analyzer — `/work/phishing-email-analyzer`

*Python · Streamlit · security · Accenture internship, 2026*

**Hook.** I tested my phishing detector against 8,612 real phishing emails. Looking at headers and content alone, it never once reached a "Phishing" verdict.

**Problem.** When someone reports a suspicious email, a SOC analyst spends the first minute on the same checks every time: does it pass authentication, are the links or IPs known-bad, and what's the verdict? That minute is repetitive and worth automating — but only if the analyst can see why the tool decided what it did.

**Insight.** Authentication checks are not enough. Across all 8,612 samples, header and content heuristics peaked at 61 out of 100, below the Phishing threshold of 70. One live credential lure passed SPF, DKIM and DMARC completely. What separates phishing from legitimate mail in practice is reputation: checking the links and IPs against live threat intelligence.

**Build.**
- A five-stage pipeline of independently tested Python modules: parse the `.eml`, extract indicators, enrich them, score, display.
- Validates SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Extracts URLs, IPs, domains and attachment hashes, and defangs every one before it is shown.
- Enriches against three live services — VirusTotal, AbuseIPDB and URLScan.io — with rate limiting, exponential backoff and caching.
- A weighted 0–100 risk score with a verdict (Legitimate / Suspicious / Phishing) and an itemised list of which signals added how many points.
- A Streamlit dashboard for upload and review, with an offline heuristics-only mode.
- Security handled as a requirement: keys in a gitignored `.env` under personal accounts, no extracted URL ever visited, attachments hashed in memory and never written to disk.

**Result.**
- Validated against **8,612** real phishing emails plus two legitimate-mail datasets.
- **17 of 17** unit tests passing.
- False-positive rate **0.4%** on 500 legitimate emails and **0.0%** on 1,000 more (content-heuristic layer).
- On a 12-email live subset, limited by API rate limits, threat-intel enrichment raised exact Phishing matches from **0% to 41.7%**, and Suspicious-or-Phishing flagging from **18.7% to 83.3%**.
- Two real evasion techniques found and documented: brand names disguised with Unicode combining marks, and phishing hosted on a legitimate Google Apps Script domain.
- A dataset turned out to contain real victims' personal data; a pre-commit check caught it before anything was staged.

### 3.3 English Winglish — `/work/english-winglish`

*React Native (Expo) · Next.js · Supabase · 2026*

**Hook.** An exam-prep app with no login screen. Students open it and are practising on the first tap.

**Problem.** Students preparing for competitive exams in Gujarat and across India — GPSC, GSSSB, SSC, Railway, Banking, Police, Talati, TET/TAT, UPSC — need English grammar practice, previous-year questions and mock tests, in both English and Gujarati. And the teacher behind the content needs to add and fix questions without calling a developer.

**Insight.** Two different users, two different products. Students get a fast mobile app with nothing in the way. The teacher gets a separate web admin panel. One database sits between them, with the rules about who can read and write enforced in the database, not just in the screens.

**Build.**
- Student app in React Native with Expo: grammar practice, previous-year questions, mock tests with a leaderboard, PDFs, videos and notes, all bilingual.
- Admin panel in Next.js for questions, content and users.
- Supabase (PostgreSQL) backend, shaped over 13 migrations. One question can serve both grammar practice and previous-year papers.
- Row-level security on every table: students read active content and their own attempts; only admins write content.
- True random question sampling, added after practice sets were being dominated by a single topic.
- Runs on bundled sample questions with no backend, so the whole interface can be explored offline.
- In-app reporting so students can flag a wrong question for the admin to resolve.

**Result.** Still in the making, with a Play Store release planned. The admin panel is deployed and an Android build exists. Built for my uncle, Nikunj Sir, whose teaching the app carries. No user numbers are claimed. *(pending: link to Nikunj Sir's YouTube channel — Rishi approved mentioning it; URL not supplied yet.)*

### 3.4 Tracker — `/work/tracker`

*React Native (Expo) · Firebase · maps · 2026*

**Hook.** One app, four different jobs: the admin sees the whole map, the driver sees today's rides, the salesman sees his leads, and the customer sees where the delivery is.

**Problem.** Plenty of businesses deliver using local autos, and send salesmen out to find new clients. The owner then has two things they can't see: how far each auto actually travelled, and how much ground each salesman covered looking for clients.

**Insight.** Tracking people is really four products sharing one set of data. Give each role only its own screens, and the app stays simple for everyone.

**Build.**
- A React Native app with Expo and file-based routing, split into four role areas — admin, driver, salesman, customer — behind a role guard.
- **Admin:** creates a ride for an auto and gets the best route based on where the parties are; live map, trips, parties, lead verification, analytics.
- **Driver:** trips and daily rides, with distance travelled tracked in the background.
- **Salesman:** adds a potential client with their location and details; the app records how far he travelled that day searching for clients.
- **Customer:** a separate login and a track screen.
- Firebase for auth and data with security rules; maps with planned-route directions; trip distance and stops calculated from the recorded track.

**Result.** A working four-role app, built solo. No usage numbers are claimed.
